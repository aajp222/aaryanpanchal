People say *I'm not a person of faith* like it is a factual statement about
their lives, and I have never once believed it.

Not because I think everyone is secretly religious. Because I think the word has
been narrowed until it only means one thing, and the thing it originally
described is happening to you constantly.

You opened your eyes and believed the floor would be where you left it. You
stood up and did not first verify gravity. You took a pill manufactured by
someone you will never meet, checked by someone else you will never meet,
carried across an ocean in the dark by a third person while you were asleep, and
you swallowed it without a moment's hesitation.

That is not recklessness. It is a functioning trust network, and you are held up
by thousands of strangers before breakfast.

## from dependence to the other thing

Now — the honest part. That argument gets you to *faith exists.* It does not get
you to *God.* I know the difference, and I am not going to pretend the second
one follows from the first, because it doesn't.

What the first one did was clear away my main objection, which was that faith
was a category of thing I did not do. It turned out faith was a category of
thing I did constantly and had simply never audited.

Once that excuse was gone, I had to actually go and look.

## what looking was like

Slow. Unspectacular. Not a lightning strike.

Reading things and not understanding them and reading them again. Asking a
question and being given an answer that was clearly the standard answer, and
having to decide whether to accept it or push. Pushing, mostly. Being the guy in
the room with the follow-up question.

Prayer, badly, for a long time — mostly a running commentary addressed slightly
upward.

<Ledger kind="question">
Does questioning this destroy it? If I keep pulling the thread does the whole
thing come apart?
</Ledger>

<Ledger kind="later">
It didn't. Questioning is what turned it from something I had been handed into
something that was mine. I have never met a faith worth having that could not
survive being asked about.
</Ledger>

## and where she was in this

Careful. This is the bit that is easy to get wrong in the retelling, in both
directions.

She was not the reason I became a Christian. I was already searching before she
existed to me — that is chapter four, and I put it before chapter five on
purpose.

But she was in the room while it happened, and it would be dishonest to pretend
the room does not matter. Faith was part of the environment we shared. We talked
about it. Some of my questions got sharper because somebody was there to ask
them out loud to.

<Ledger kind="fact">
I was searching before I met her. I was still searching after.
</Ledger>

<Ledger kind="interpretation">
For a while I let the two get braided together, which meant that when one came
apart I was afraid the other would too.
</Ledger>

<Ledger kind="later">
The braid was mine. I made it. Untangling it was the hardest and most necessary
work in this entire book.
</Ledger>
