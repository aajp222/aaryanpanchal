## the fine print I had been hiding from myself

For about a year I had been telling anyone who asked — and telling myself, more
often — that I had let her go.

Here is what I now think I actually meant:

<Break>
  <Monument size="huge">
    I let you go,<br />
    <em className="italic">as long as you stay mine</em><br />
    in some invisible way.
  </Monument>
</Break>

That is not release. That is a longer leash with better branding.

You can test it, and the test is unpleasant. It is not *did you stop contacting
her.* It is: what happens in your chest when she is happy in a way that has
nothing to do with you? If the answer is anything other than *good*, then the
release was conditional, and a conditional release is not one.

Mine was extremely conditional. Chapter seventeen is the receipt.

<Piece id="trying" />

## what releasing actually includes

Here is the part I did not want and could not get around.

If I release someone, they are actually free. Not free within a range I find
acceptable. Free — including free to choose somebody who is not me. Including
free to be *better off*. Including free to not think about this as much as I do.

If any of those outcomes is intolerable to me, then what I have is not love with
open hands. It is ownership with good manners.

<Ledger kind="feeling">
I hate this. All of this. I would like there to be a version where letting go is
noble and then rewarded.
</Ledger>

<Ledger kind="fact">
There isn't. That is the version where you let go in order to get something,
which is the same grip with a longer time horizon.
</Ledger>

<Ledger kind="prayer">
Then help me want her to be well in a way that does not require me to be in the
room for it.
</Ledger>

<Ledger kind="later">
That prayer took months to become true and it is not permanently true. It is a
thing I have to mean again, on purpose, at intervals.
</Ledger>

## not the cliché

There is a sentence about this that everyone knows and I refuse to write, because
it is used almost exclusively by people who have not done it, as a way of
sounding wise about a thing they are actually still gripping.

What I will say is the smaller, less quotable version:

Letting go is not loving someone less. The quantity does not change. What changes
is the *shape* — from a closed hand to an open one, holding exactly the same
thing, with the enormous difference that it can now leave.
