## the order of events matters

I had just finished convincing myself that a person was not what I was missing.
I had put the whole category down. I was, for the first time in years, genuinely
not scanning a room.

And then there was a conversation. And then there was another one.

<Piece id="we-talked" />

I resisted it. I want that on the record because it is the part people skip when
they retell this kind of story. I did not fall gratefully into it. I had *just*
gotten somewhere, and this felt like being handed back the exact thing I had
finally set down.

I remember thinking: this is a test, and the correct answer is no.

I was wrong about what the test was.

## what it was actually made of

Not fireworks. I keep waiting for the fireworks part of this memory and it is
not there.

It was: talking until it was late in a way that did not feel like staying up.
Classrooms. Terrible coffee. Someone laughing at the specific stupid thing I
said, not at a general funny thing. Walking somewhere slowly on purpose so it
would take longer.

The particular sunlight in the afternoon on that side of campus.

<Piece id="found-not-introduced" />

## what I did not understand yet

I thought what was happening was that I had found a person.

What was actually happening — and I could not have told you this at the time, I
am only able to say it now, from the other end — is that I was in the middle of
looking for God, and something arrived that made me feel *known*, and I did not
yet have the equipment to tell those two things apart.

<Ledger kind="fact">
I met someone while I was searching for God.
</Ledger>

<Ledger kind="feeling">
It felt like an answer. It felt like the search resolving.
</Ledger>

<Ledger kind="interpretation">
So I concluded that she was what the searching had been for.
</Ledger>

<Ledger kind="later">
She was not the answer, and it is not a demotion to say so. Being loved by
someone taught me what it feels like to be known, which is the thing I had been
looking for a word for. She was not the destination. She was the first evidence
that the destination existed.
</Ledger>

This chapter is the lightest one in the book. I have thought about whether to
weigh it down with what comes later — to hedge it, to write it already knowing
how it ends.

I've decided not to. It was good. It was actually, genuinely good, and it would
be a small and cowardly kind of revisionism to go back and dim it now because of
what happened afterwards.

It gets to stay bright.
