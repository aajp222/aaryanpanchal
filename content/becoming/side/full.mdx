Food. Family. Affection. Trust.

That is the complete list of what my dog requires, and she is — as far as I can
observe, and I have observed closely, at length, at an hour I should have been
asleep — completely full.

Not resigned. Not making the best of a limited situation. *Full.*

<Piece id="period" />

## the thing that gets me

When I come home, she behaves as though my return is the single largest event to
have occurred anywhere since the last time it happened.

The same reaction. Every time. Undiminished by repetition, uncorrected by the
overwhelming statistical evidence that I come back basically always.

I have thought about this more than is normal.

Because I do the opposite, constantly, at scale. I get the thing I spent a year
wanting and I feel roughly nine minutes of it and then I am scanning for the
next one. My capacity to be delighted by a recurring good thing degrades on
contact.

<Ledger kind="question">
Is she content because her list is short, or is her list short because she is
content?
</Ledger>

<Ledger kind="later">
I don't think it's either. I think she has no mechanism for comparison, and
comparison is where almost all of my dissatisfaction is manufactured.
</Ledger>

## what I am not saying

I am not saying be a dog. That's the cheap version and it isn't available to us
anyway — I have a frontal lobe and plans and a company to build, and I do not
actually want to give those up.

And ambition is not the enemy here. I like wanting things. Some of the best
things about my life came directly out of wanting them badly.

The question isn't *want less.* The question is the one from chapter three that
I still cannot answer:

<Break>
  <Monument size="huge">
    How much is <em className="italic">enough?</em>
  </Monument>
</Break>

<Piece id="am-i-full" />

She puts her head on my foot and ends the sentence of my day, and she has no
idea she is the most persuasive philosophical argument in the house.
