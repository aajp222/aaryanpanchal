I look at trees more than I used to. I don't fully know when that started.

There is one on the way to the lab that I have now walked past several hundred
times, and I could not have told you a single thing about it for the first
hundred.

## growth you cannot watch

This is the part that gets me.

You cannot observe a tree growing. Not with attention, not with patience, not by
standing there. The process is real, continuous, and permanently below the
threshold of the instrument you are using.

You can only ever observe *that it has grown*, retroactively, by comparison, and
usually by accident — an old photograph, a branch that now reaches something it
did not used to reach.

I find that reassuring and slightly unbearable in equal measure, mostly because
I have spent this entire website trying to check whether I am getting better yet.

<Ledger kind="question">
Am I actually different, or am I just describing myself more articulately?
</Ledger>

<Ledger kind="later">
Unanswerable from inside. The only evidence available to me is the occasional
old photograph — something I wrote two years ago that I now disagree with, a
situation I handled a way I would not have handled it before.
</Ledger>

## standing

Trees don't get to leave. That's the constraint the whole organism is designed
around.

They cannot relocate away from a bad season, or a neighbour, or a drought. So
everything they have is adaptation in place — deeper, tougher, differently
shaped, dropping what cannot be carried through winter.

I spent a long time believing the answer to a hard season was to *go*. Different
school, different city, different version of myself, keep the calendar full
enough that nothing can catch up.

Some of the most useful growth I have done happened in a season I could not
leave. Same campus. Same friend groups. Same rooms as someone I could not be
around comfortably yet.

Nowhere to go, so the only available direction was down.
