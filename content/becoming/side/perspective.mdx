One of them finds comfort in it. The other finds a demand. A third has heard it
so many times at so many funerals that it has gone smooth and means nothing at
all any more.

None of them misread it.

## how I used to handle this

Badly. I used to treat it as a bug.

If a text means different things to different people, I reasoned, then either
some of them are wrong, or the text does not really mean anything, and either
way somebody should sort this out. I wanted a correct reading. I wanted to know
which team had it right, and — this is the embarrassing part — I mostly wanted
to know so that I could be on it.

<Ledger kind="memory">
Twelve years old, Hindu, at monthly Mass in a Catholic school, singing along
happily, having been informed by nobody that these were separate teams.
</Ledger>

<Ledger kind="memory">
Twenty years old, fluent in the divisions. Denominations, sects, which practice
is objectionable to whom, who may marry whom.
</Ledger>

<Ledger kind="interpretation">
My own view, held loosely and offered as mine rather than as a fact about the
universe: God did not hand me those divisions. People did — often for reasons
that made sense at the time, in circumstances I have not had to live in.
</Ledger>

<Ledger kind="question">
And is that just a convenient position for someone standing between two
traditions who would rather not choose?
</Ledger>

<Ledger kind="later">
Maybe. I hold it anyway, and I try to hold it without the smugness that usually
comes attached to it.
</Ledger>

## why a sentence lands differently

Because it does not land in a vacuum. It lands in whatever is already in the
room.

Your father is in the room. What happened to you in March is in the room. The
language you dream in is in the room. Whether you are, at that moment, the one
being comforted or the one being asked for something.

The words are fixed. The room is not. Meaning is what happens when one meets the
other, which means meaning is partly *made of you* — and that is not a flaw in
the text, that is the mechanism by which anything written thousands of years ago
can still do work today.
