Here is what I thought love was, before: an intensity. A feeling with a high
ceiling. Something you would recognise by its *volume*.

Here is what it actually was.

Knowing which sauce. Knowing the order before it gets said. Knowing which
complaints are real complaints and which ones are just weather. Knowing the
laugh that means something is genuinely funny versus the laugh that means she is
being polite to someone.

Being the person she calls when something stupid happens. Not something bad —
something *stupid*. Anyone can be called about a crisis. Being the stupid-thing
call is a much higher clearance.

<Piece id="the-fall-you-didnt-brace" />

## peace, in a loud place

That line took me a long time to get to and it is the truest thing I have
written about her.

Not her face. I want to be precise, because "not for her face" reads like a
humblebrag if you skim it. What I mean is: the thing I was actually attached to
was not an attribute she possessed. It was a *state I was in* when she was
around.

My head is loud. It has been loud since I was seven. And there was a period
where it was, for whole hours at a time, not loud.

That is what I was in love with. Which is worth knowing, because it turns out
that is a thing you can lose access to without losing the person, and a thing
you can keep after the person is gone.

## it was also extremely stupid

I do not want this chapter to be reverent, because it wasn't.

We were ridiculous. There were whole conversations that were just escalating
nonsense. There was a running argument about food that neither of us was
actually trying to win. There were nicknames that would be genuinely
embarrassing to explain to a third party and which I am therefore not going to
explain to you.

<Piece id="love-is-like-a-fart" />

I stand by it. Both the poem and the position.

## the part that lasted

The small knowledge is the part I still have.

Nobody warns you about this. When something ends, you assume the big things go
first and the small things go with them. It's the reverse. The big things —
the plans, the shape of the future — those evaporate almost immediately, because
they were always hypothetical.

What stays is that I still know the sauce.

<Ledger kind="later">
It isn't a haunting. It's just what intimacy leaves behind. The knowledge was
the love. It didn't stop being real when the arrangement stopped being possible.
</Ledger>
