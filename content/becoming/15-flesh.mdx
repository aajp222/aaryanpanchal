I want to describe what that was like precisely, because *a verse spoke to me*
is the kind of sentence that means nothing to anyone who hasn't had it happen.

It was not comfort. It was recognition, and it was uncomfortable.

Somebody had written down, thousands of years before I existed, in a language I
do not read, the *exact object* I had been carrying since I was seven. Not a
metaphor near it. The thing itself. A heart of stone, in your flesh, that has to
come out.

I had made that sentence up. I was seven and I made it up in Hindi in my
father's car and I had been quietly proud of it for fifteen years, and it turned
out I had not invented anything. I had described a condition so common that it
already had a name.

## what a heart of flesh actually is

Here is the part I got wrong for a long time, and I got it wrong in the
flattering direction.

I assumed *heart of flesh* was the reward. Stone is the hard part, flesh is the
healed part, you go through the difficulty and you arrive somewhere softer and
better and it stops hurting.

That is not what flesh is.

Flesh bruises. Flesh is the part of you that can be cut. A heart of flesh is not
a heart that has stopped being hurt — it is a heart that has lost the ability to
*not* be. That is the whole trade. You give up the armour and what you get back
is not safety. What you get back is everything the armour was keeping out, which
includes all of it, the good and the rest.

<Break>
  <Monument size="huge">
    A heart of flesh can hurt.<br />
    That is not the <em className="italic">flaw.</em><br />

It can hurt. It can cry — I have evidence, one chapter back, after fifteen years
of none. It can trust. It can forgive. It can change shape under load. It can
love something without owning it.

And the last one, which is the whole reason this website exists:

## the thing I used to believe

I used to think strength meant becoming impossible to hurt. I built for that
requirement for fifteen years and I was genuinely good at it. People
complimented me on it. It read as maturity.

I now think that was never strength. It was a very well-engineered absence.

Strength — the version I am currently working with, and I reserve the right to
revise it — is staying soft enough to love someone after you already have
evidence about what that costs.

Not *despite* the evidence. With the evidence. Having read it. Choosing anyway.

<Pause size="small" />

That is not a conclusion. It is much harder than a conclusion, because a
conclusion is something you reach once, and this is something that has to be
decided again roughly every few weeks, usually at an inconvenient time, usually
when the stone would be so much easier.

The chapters after this one are the record of me failing that test, and then
passing it, and then failing it again.
