I have written this chapter several times looking for the villain, because a
villain would be so much easier to hold than what actually happened.

There isn't one.

There were two families with two entirely coherent sets of expectations. There
were two cultures that each had a long, considered, internally consistent answer
to the question of who a person marries and what a family owes. There was
religion, on both sides, load-bearing on both sides. There was secrecy, which we
chose, and which cost more than we understood at the time.

And there were two people in the middle who loved each other and were nineteen
and twenty and had no leverage over any of it.

## what I had to learn

I genuinely believed, going in, that love was a solvent. That a sufficient
quantity of it would eventually dissolve the obstacles, because that is what
every story I had ever been told said would happen.

<Break>
  <Monument size="large">
    love ≠ compatibility<br />
    love ≠ permission<br />
    love ≠ timing
  </Monument>
</Break>

Those are three separate axes and you can score full marks on the first one and
zero on the others, and the relationship still does not run. That is not a
tragedy with a moral. That is just what it is like when a real thing meets a
real structure.

<Ledger kind="fact">
We separated.
</Ledger>

<Ledger kind="feeling">
That if I had been better — smarter, more convincing, more patient, more
something — this would have gone differently.
</Ledger>

<Ledger kind="later">
That feeling is a form of grandiosity dressed up as guilt. It assumes I had
enough power over the situation to have changed it. I didn't. Almost nobody
ever does, at twenty.
</Ledger>

## the secrecy

One thing I will say against us both: the secrecy did real damage, and it was
the one variable we actually controlled.

Keeping something hidden puts it in a room with no windows. Nothing gets in — no
advice, no friction, no one's honest opinion, nobody saying *have you considered*.
And nothing gets out either.

I have written that sentence before, in this book, about something else.

## it did not end cleanly

It did not end with a scene. There was no door.

There was a decision, and then a slow decompression, and then a period of
several months where the two of us were technically not in each other's lives
and functionally still entirely in them.

That period does not get a chapter, because I could not tell you what happened
in it. I was there and I could not tell you.
