Nobody leaves. That's the thing about this kind of ending in a place like a
university. There is no moving away, no clean geographic break, no *and then she
moved to another city and it faded.*

Same campus. Same friend groups, mostly. Same handful of buildings. Same
meetings, occasionally.

## what I actually missed

This surprised me and I think it surprises most people, so I want to be specific
about it.

It was not the romance. It was not the physical part. Those are the things
people assume, and they are the things that are easiest to talk about, and they
are not what kept me up.

<Piece id="you-will-not-believe" />

That's it. That's the whole grief.

Something mildly ridiculous happens to you on a Tuesday — the kind of thing that
is not a story, that would bore anybody, that is only funny because of eleven
previous conversations — and you reach for your phone with it already half
composed, and there is nowhere to put it.

The thing you lose is not a person. The person is fine, the person is over there
by the door. The thing you lose is a *destination*. An address where the small
stuff went.

<Ledger kind="memory">
Reaching for the phone maybe forty times before the reflex stopped.
</Ledger>

<Ledger kind="feeling">
Embarrassed, mostly. It felt like a disproportionate thing to be undone by.
</Ledger>

<Ledger kind="later">
It isn't disproportionate. The small stuff was the actual substance of it. Of
course losing the small stuff is what it feels like.
</Ledger>

## being courteous at someone

The specific texture of this period was politeness.

We were kind to each other. Genuinely — this is not sarcasm. There was no
hostility, no scene, no mutual-friends-choosing-sides. When we spoke it was
warm. Someone watching would have said *those two get on fine.*

And it was awful, in a low, ambient, non-dramatic way, because every one of
those interactions required me to perform a version of myself who had less
information than I had. To be pleasantly unaware of four hundred small facts.

That is what I meant by ghost. Not tragic — *present without effect*. There, and
unable to act on any of it.
