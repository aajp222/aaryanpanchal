I'm not being coy. I genuinely do not know, I will not know for a long time, and
I have stopped organising my life around finding out.

If you came here for the resolution, this is the chapter where I disappoint you
on purpose. Not out of mystery — out of accuracy. I am twenty-something years
old and in the middle of it, and any ending I wrote for you right now would be
a thing I made up to make you comfortable.

## plasticity

I do this for a living, sort of, so let me use the vocabulary I actually have.

A material under load does one of three things. It springs back — elastic, no
memory, nothing learned. It breaks — brittle, all at once, usually without
warning. Or it deforms and *stays* deformed, carries the load in its new shape,
and does not fail.

That third one is plasticity, and in every materials class it gets taught as a
kind of damage. Permanent deformation. A part that will not return to spec.

I have come to think it is the best thing a material can do.

## the three things

I cannot change what happened. I cannot change what two families believe, or
what she decides, or what somebody I have never met does next, or whether any of
this ever gets a second act.

<Break>
  <Monument size="huge">
    my reaction.<br />
    my interaction.<br />

That is the full list. Everything else is somebody else's variable, and most of
my suffering in this story came from trying to apply force to variables that
were never mine.

## what everything taught me

This looked like a story about a girl for about eight chapters. It isn't. She is
one teacher in it, and not the only one, and it would be unfair to her and
untrue to me to make one person responsible for a transformation this large.

<Ledger kind="later" label="What taught me what">
India taught me goodbye. My parents taught me that people can do an enormous
thing quietly and never mention the cost. A dog taught me the difference between
wanting and needing. Engineering taught me that constraints are what make a
system work, and that failure is data. She taught me what it is to be known.
Losing her taught me what I had actually been holding. And God became the thing
all of them started pointing at, rather than one more item on the list.
</Ledger>

## arrived?

<Piece id="arrived" />

<Pause size="small" />

I am not going to write a conclusion, because a conclusion would be a lie about
the state of the thing.

I used to believe strength meant becoming impossible to hurt.

I now believe — provisionally, revisably, on the days I can manage it — that
strength means staying soft enough to love again after you have the evidence
about what that costs.

I get that wrong regularly. There are weeks where the stone is genuinely
appealing, where it would be so much more efficient, where I can feel the old
architecture offering itself.

So far, I have not gone back.
