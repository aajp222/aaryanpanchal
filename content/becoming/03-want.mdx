Some of it was good wanting. I wanted to build things. I wanted to understand
how a mechanism moves before I was allowed to touch it. I wanted to be the
person in the room who had actually read the thing.

And some of it was just volume.

I wanted the grade and the internship and the title and the attention and the
girlfriend, and I wanted them roughly in that order and roughly all at once, and
if you had asked me why I would have given you a very good answer that was not
the real one.

The real one was: I thought there was an amount that would do it. I thought
somewhere out there was a quantity of achieved things that would finally make
the low hum stop.

<Crowded />

## the dog

We have a dog. I want to be careful here because *dogs are better than people*
is a lazy thing to say and I don't believe it.

But watching her did something to me.

She has food. She has us. She gets scratched behind the ear. That is the entire
inventory. And she is not *coping* with that — she is not making do — she
genuinely appears to be at capacity. Full. Nothing pending.

And when I come home, she reacts like my return is the single largest event to
have occurred in the universe since the last time I came home, which was
Tuesday.

Then she lies down and puts her head on my foot.

<Piece id="period" />

## the question I could not get out of

I am not saying be a dog. I am saying I could not answer her.

I could not explain, to myself, in a way that held up, why my list was so long
and hers was so short and she was so obviously fine.

<Ledger kind="question">
How much is enough? Not rhetorically. Numerically. What is the actual number of
achieved things at which the hum stops?
</Ledger>

<Ledger kind="fact">
Every time I got one of the things on the list, the list got longer.
</Ledger>

<Ledger kind="interpretation">
At the time I read that as ambition, and I was proud of it.
</Ledger>

<Ledger kind="later">
A system where the target moves every time you reach it is not ambition. In
engineering we call that an unstable loop, and we fix it, because it will tear
itself apart.
</Ledger>

<Piece id="am-i-full" />

I want to be honest about where this chapter ends, which is: not resolved. I
still want too much. I still catch myself pricing my own week in outputs.

But I did, somewhere in here, stop believing the arithmetic. I stopped believing
that the number existed.

Which meant I had to start asking what I was actually missing.
