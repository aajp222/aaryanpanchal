I was seven. Maybe eight. I said it to my father, in the language we used for
the things that actually mattered, and I remember being proud of it. It sounded
like something a strong person would say.

Before that, leaving India used to wreck me. Every single time. The airport, the
gate, my grandmother doing the smile people do so you don't see the other thing
happening on their face. I would cry the whole way to security and then some of
the way across the Atlantic, and I was not quiet about it.

Then my parents separated, and I stayed in India for about six months while the
adults worked out what the rest of our life was going to look like. Nobody
explained very much. I don't hold that against them. I've since learned that
people in the middle of something rarely have a spare hand free to narrate it.

Somewhere in those six months, I stopped crying.

<Ledger kind="memory">
I stopped crying. It wasn't a decision I remember making.
</Ledger>

<Ledger kind="feeling">
I felt strong. Genuinely — not brave-face strong. Strong like something had been
installed.
</Ledger>

<Ledger kind="interpretation">
So I decided that was what strength *was*. Not feeling it. Not being reachable.
</Ledger>

<Ledger kind="later">
Those are three different sentences, and it took me about fifteen years to
notice that I had been treating them as one.
</Ledger>

## a child needs a rule

An adult gets to say *this is complicated and it will hurt for a while and then
it will hurt differently.* A child can't hold that. A child needs a rule, and
the rule has to be short enough to remember while something is happening to you.

Mine was:

<Break>
  <Monument size="huge">
    stone is strong.<br />
    stone does not cry.<br />
    if nothing gets in,<br />
    <em className="italic">nothing can hurt you.</em>
  </Monument>
</Break>

It's not a bad rule. That's the part people miss when they hear it. It is a
genuinely excellent rule, for a while, if what you need is to get through the
next six months of a thing nobody is explaining to you.

<Stone />

## it worked

That's why it lasted. Nothing survives fifteen years inside a person unless it
is doing something useful.

I barely cried after that. Not at goodbyes, not at funerals, not at the things
where everyone around me had clearly been issued equipment I hadn't. I was
reliable. I was calm in rooms that were not calm. People told me I was mature,
and I collected it like a grade.

I got good at being the one who was fine.

<Pause size="small" />

It would be a long time before anyone pointed out the other half of the
sentence — that a thing which cannot be entered also cannot be filled, and a
thing that cannot crack cannot be gotten out of.

<Piece id="mera-dil-patthar" />

Everything after this is what happened to that stone.
