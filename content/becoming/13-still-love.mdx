<Pause />

I need you to sit with that for a second, because I could not, for a long time.

Every framework I had been handed said that if the love is real, the love is the
answer. That the obstacles are a test *of* the love, and sufficient love clears
them. That if it didn't work out, one of us must not have loved enough, and
therefore there is a person to be angry at, and therefore there is something to
*do*.

None of that was true here.

The love was in perfect working order. It had survived months of silence, a
separation neither of us wanted, and eleven weeks of aggressively polite group
work. It was, if anything, in better condition than it had been.

And it changed *nothing.*

<Piece id="almost" />

## the arithmetic

Same families. Same expectations. Same two traditions, each with a long
considered answer. Same ages, same lack of leverage, same geography, same
everything.

We had not solved a single structural problem. We had only established that the
feeling was still running underneath all of them, which turns out to be
information about us and not information about the situation.

<Ledger kind="fact">
Two people still loved each other. Every reason it could not work was still
in place.
</Ledger>

<Ledger kind="feeling">
Cheated. Not by her — by the shape of it. Like being shown the answer and told
it doesn't count.
</Ledger>

<Ledger kind="question">
Then what is love actually for, if it can be completely real and completely
insufficient at the same time?
</Ledger>

<Ledger kind="later">
Love is not a solvent and it was never advertised as one. It is not a tool for
changing circumstances. It is a way of being toward a person. I had been using
the wrong instrument and blaming it for the reading.
</Ledger>

## what this cost

I want to name the specific thing that hurt, because *breakup* is too blunt a
word and it isn't what this was.

The thing that hurt was learning that being right about a person, and being
right about how you feel, and both of you being right at the same time, is not
enough. That the universe does not owe you an outcome for having gotten the
hardest part correct.

That is not a lesson about her. That is a lesson about the world, and it is one
of maybe three genuinely load-bearing things I have learned so far.
