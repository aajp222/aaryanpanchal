Nobody betrayed me.

I need to open with that, because everything in this chapter is downstream of it
and if you lose it the chapter turns into something ugly that I do not mean.

There was no lie I can point to. There was no promise broken that I could hold
up and read out. Two people separated, properly, with apologies in both
directions, and then some months later one of them was seen laughing near
somebody else, which she was entirely free to do, having been — at my own
insistence, in my own words — let go.

And something broke anyway.

## what I had actually been trusting

It took me a while to work out what the broken thing even was, because it
clearly was not a promise.

Here it is: I had been trusting a *shared interpretation*. I believed we were
both walking around inside the same story — that we had both lost the same
thing, that we were both grieving it on roughly the same schedule, that the
separation was a shared wound rather than two separate ones.

That was never agreed to. Nobody offered it and nobody accepted it. I assembled
it myself, out of a phone call at four in the morning and my own need for the
loss to be symmetrical.

<Ledger kind="fact">
Two people ended a relationship. One of them was later seen with someone else.
</Ledger>

<Ledger kind="feeling">
That the story I had been living in was not the story she had been living in.
</Ledger>

<Ledger kind="interpretation">
Which I initially processed as: she moved on faster, therefore she felt less,
therefore it meant less, therefore I was a fool.
</Ledger>

<Ledger kind="later">
Grief has no standard rate. Two people can lose the identical thing and be at
completely different points eight months later, and neither number tells you
anything about how much it mattered. I was reading a stopwatch as a scale.
</Ledger>

## trust is a load path

The thing about trust is that it is invisible while it is working. This is not a
poetic observation, it's a structural one.

Nobody in this building is thinking about the floor. The floor is carrying every
one of us right now and it has never once entered our attention, and it will not,
unless it moves. Attention is not how we relate to the things holding us up.
Attention is what happens *after* they fail.

<Break>
  <Monument size="large">
    We notice trust<br />
    most clearly at the exact moment<br />
    it stops being <em className="italic">true.</em>
  </Monument>
</Break>

Which means — and this is the uncomfortable part — most of the trust in your
life is currently unexamined by definition. You cannot go and check it. Checking
it is not a thing you can do without changing it.

## where that leaves me

Somewhere honest, I think.

I do not believe she wronged me. I have gone looking for it, in bad faith,
several times, and it isn't there.

What I believe is that I built a structure on an assumption I never verified,
and when the assumption turned out not to be load-bearing, the structure came
down, and that is my engineering error and not her fault.

<Ledger kind="question">
So was any of it real?
</Ledger>

<Ledger kind="later">
Yes. That question only feels like a trap because I was treating *real* and
*permanent* as the same word. They have never been the same word. Chapter
thirteen already told me that and I had to learn it twice.
</Ledger>
